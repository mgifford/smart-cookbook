Recipe

Broccoli and Basil Soup

Source

Servings
4
Prep
25 min
Dietary
Vegetarian: not recorded · Vegan: not recorded
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes

Ingredients

  • 1 tsp — olive oil
  • 1 large onion
  • 1 large potato — potato, peeled and chopped
  • 3 sticks — celery stalks, chopped
  • 1 large head — broccoli, roughly chopped
  • 2 cloves — garlic cloves, finely diced
  • 500 ml (2 cups) — reduced-salt vegetable stock
  • 800 ml (3.5 cups) — boiling water
  • 25g pack (about 1 cup packed) — fresh basil (including stalks)
  • 1/4 tsp (to taste) — black pepper

Directions

  1. Heat the olive oil over medium heat in a large pan. Add the chopped onion, potato, and celery. Cook for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are soft and the onion is translucent.
  2. Add the roughly chopped broccoli (including stems), diced garlic, vegetable stock, and boiling water. Stir to combine.
  3. Bring to the boil, then reduce the heat and simmer for 10 minutes, or until the broccoli and all vegetables are cooked through and tender.
  4. Stir through most of the fresh basil (including stalks), saving a few leaves to garnish. The heat will wilt the basil and release its aromatic oils.
  5. Remove from heat and let cool slightly (about 5 minutes). Blend the soup using an immersion blender directly in the pot, or carefully transfer in batches to a regular blender and blend until smooth.
  6. Return the soup to the pan to reheat if necessary. Taste and season with freshly ground black pepper to taste.
  7. Divide between 4 bowls and garnish with the remaining fresh basil leaves before serving. Serve with toasted baguette rubbed with a garlic clove for a healthy spin on garlic bread.
Why this works
  • Sautéing vegetables at medium heat activates enzymes that break down cell walls, concentrating flavors through caramelization without browning.
  • Celery contains 3-n-butylphthalide, an aromatic compound that gives it distinctive flavor and may help lower blood pressure.
  • Broccoli contains sulforaphane, a potent antioxidant formed when the enzyme myrosinase (released by chopping) contacts glucoraphanin. Both cooking and blending preserve this compound.
  • Garlic's allicin is formed when crushing/chopping releases enzyme alliinase. Adding garlic late prevents it from burning (which creates bitterness).
  • Potato starch (amylose and amylopectin) gelatinizes when heated in liquid, swelling to create natural creaminess when pureed without dairy.
  • Simmering at 180-200°F gently breaks down vegetable cell walls without aggressive boiling that can leach nutrients into cooking water.
  • Fresh basil contains volatile oils (linalool, eugenol) that provide sweet, peppery flavor. Blending basil releases these oils, infusing the soup.
  • Basil stalks contain concentrated flavor compounds and nutrients. Using the whole plant reduces waste and maximizes taste.
  • Blending hot soup creates an emulsion—tiny vegetable particles suspended in liquid—that feels rich and creamy even with minimal fat.
  • Black pepper contains piperine, which enhances nutrient absorption (especially from cruciferous vegetables) and adds mild heat without masking other flavors.
  • This soup freezes well because the emulsified structure remains stable when thawed. Cruciferous vegetables retain nutrients even after freezing.

What these labels mean

Vegetarian means no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. Cheeses can use animal rennet, so check labels if that matters to you.

Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients at all, including dairy, eggs, honey, gelatin, and animal rennet. A vegetarian recipe is not automatically vegan.

These are guidance labels, not certification. Ingredient brands and processing aids vary.
