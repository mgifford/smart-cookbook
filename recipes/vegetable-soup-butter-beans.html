Recipe

Vegetable Soup with Butter Beans

Source

Servings
6
Prep
60 min
Dietary
Vegetarian: not recorded · Vegan: not recorded
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes

Ingredients

  • 1 tbsp — olive or vegetable oil
  • 3 cups (mixed) — diced vegetables (potatoes, carrots, or whatever available)
  • 1 tbsp — all-purpose flour
  • 2 cubes — chicken stock cubes
  • 3 cups — water
  • 1 tbsp — tomato paste
  • 400g can (about 1.5 cups) — butter beans, canned (drained and rinsed)
  • 1/2 cup — milk
  • 1/2 cup — cream (heavy cream or thickened cream)
  • 1/2 cup (uncooked) — white rice

Directions

  1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium heat. Add the diced vegetables and fry for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they begin to soften and develop some color.
  2. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir well for 1-2 minutes. This creates a roux that will thicken the soup and prevent lumps.
  3. Crumble the chicken stock cubes into the pan, then add the water, tomato paste, and drained butter beans. Stir to combine, making sure the flour is fully incorporated.
  4. Bring the mixture to a boil over high heat, then reduce to medium-low and simmer for 30 minutes, or until the vegetables are completely tender.
  5. Remove from heat and let cool slightly (about 5 minutes). Blend the soup in a blender or food processor until smooth. Work in batches if necessary, filling the blender only halfway.
  6. Return the pureed soup to the saucepan. Add the milk, cream, and uncooked rice. Stir to combine.
  7. Bring back to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat and cook for 15-20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the rice is tender and fully cooked.
  8. Taste and adjust seasoning with salt and pepper if needed. Serve hot.
Why this works
  • Sautéing vegetables in oil before adding liquid develops flavor through the Maillard reaction (browning of proteins and sugars).
  • Flour cooked in fat creates a roux—the foundation for thickened soups and sauces. Cooking flour removes raw, starchy taste.
  • Stirring flour into hot oil coats the starch granules with fat, preventing clumping when liquid is added.
  • Stock cubes are concentrated flavor (hydrolyzed proteins, yeast extract, salt). They dissolve and distribute umami throughout the soup.
  • Tomato paste is concentrated tomato solids (about 30% vs 5% in fresh tomatoes). Cooking it caramelizes sugars and deepens flavor.
  • Butter beans (lima beans) are 20% starch by weight. When pureed, they create natural creaminess and body without dairy.
  • Simmering vegetables for 30 minutes breaks down cell walls and releases pectin, which adds body and helps thicken the soup.
  • Blending soup creates an emulsion of starch, protein, and fat suspended in liquid—resulting in a creamy texture even before adding dairy.
  • Rice added after blending absorbs liquid and releases starch granules, further thickening the soup and adding texture.
  • Milk and cream add richness and round out flavors. Their fat molecules coat the palate, creating a luxurious mouthfeel.
  • This soup is nutrient-dense and easily digestible—ideal for people recovering from illness or undergoing cancer treatment who need gentle, nourishing calories.
  • Butter beans provide plant-based protein (7g per cup) and fiber, supporting gut health and satiety.

What these labels mean

Vegetarian means no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. Cheeses can use animal rennet, so check labels if that matters to you.

Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients at all, including dairy, eggs, honey, gelatin, and animal rennet. A vegetarian recipe is not automatically vegan.

These are guidance labels, not certification. Ingredient brands and processing aids vary.
