Recipe

Smooth Vegetable Soup

Based on Full Frontal Ostomy description

Servings
4
Prep
40 min
Dietary
Vegetarian: not recorded · Vegan: not recorded
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes

Ingredients

  • 2 medium carrots — carrots, peeled and sliced
  • 1 medium potato — Yukon gold potato, peeled and diced
  • 1 small zucchini (optional) — zucchini, peeled and chopped
  • 1 stalk (optional) — celery stalk, peeled and finely chopped
  • 4 cups — low-sodium vegetable or chicken broth
  • 3 oz (optional for protein) — lean ground turkey or chicken
  • 1 tbsp — olive oil or unsalted butter
  • 1/2 tsp (to taste, use sparingly) — salt
  • pinch (optional mild seasoning) — dried thyme or parsley

Directions

  1. Heat olive oil or butter in a large pot over medium heat. If using ground turkey or chicken, add it now and cook until no longer pink, breaking it into small pieces with a wooden spoon (about 5-7 minutes). Remove cooked meat and set aside.
  2. Add carrots, potato, zucchini (if using), and celery (if using) to the pot. Sauté for 3-4 minutes to soften slightly and develop flavor.
  3. Pour in the broth and bring to a boil. Reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for 20-25 minutes, or until all vegetables are very tender and easily pierced with a fork.
  4. If you used ground turkey or chicken, return it to the pot. Add the dried thyme or parsley (if using) and salt to taste.
  5. Remove from heat and let cool slightly (about 5 minutes). Use an immersion blender to puree the soup until completely smooth. Alternatively, carefully transfer in batches to a regular blender and blend until smooth, then return to the pot.
  6. Taste and adjust seasoning as needed. If the soup is too thick, add more broth or water to reach desired consistency. Reheat gently if needed before serving.
Why this works
  • Browning ground meat (Maillard reaction) creates savory flavor compounds. Breaking meat into small pieces increases surface area for browning and makes blending easier.
  • Sautéing vegetables before adding liquid activates enzymes and begins breaking down cell walls, which concentrates flavors and reduces cooking time.
  • Simmering (180-200°F) gently breaks down vegetable cell walls and gelatinizes potato starch, which thickens the soup naturally when pureed.
  • Potatoes contain amylose and amylopectin starches that swell when heated in liquid, creating a creamy texture when blended without needing cream.
  • Pureeing vegetables creates an emulsion—tiny particles suspended in liquid—that feels smooth and rich on the palate even with minimal fat.
  • Peeling vegetables removes insoluble fiber and bitter compounds in skins, creating a smoother, milder flavor profile suitable for sensitive digestion.
  • Salt enhances flavor by suppressing bitterness and enhancing sweet/savory notes. Add after cooking to maintain control over sodium levels, especially with low-sodium broth.
  • Cooling soup slightly before blending reduces steam pressure and prevents hot liquid from erupting. Always blend hot liquids carefully or in small batches.

What these labels mean

Vegetarian means no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. Cheeses can use animal rennet, so check labels if that matters to you.

Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients at all, including dairy, eggs, honey, gelatin, and animal rennet. A vegetarian recipe is not automatically vegan.

These are guidance labels, not certification. Ingredient brands and processing aids vary.
