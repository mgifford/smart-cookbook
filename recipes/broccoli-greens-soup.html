Recipe

Broccoli & Greens Soup

Source

Servings
4
Prep
15 min
Cook
1 hr
Dietary
Vegetarian: not recorded · Vegan: not recorded
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes

Ingredients

  • 12 whole cloves — garlic cloves, peeled and trimmed
  • 1 large onion — yellow onion, roughly chopped
  • 1/2 large head — cauliflower, sliced into florets
  • 1 head — broccoli, sliced into florets
  • 3 tbsp (divided) — extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp — dried parsley
  • 1 tsp — dried basil
  • 1 tsp — dried oregano
  • 1/4 tsp — dried rosemary
  • 1/4 tsp — dried thyme
  • 1 tsp — sea salt
  • 8 cups (about 1 bunch, stems removed) — kale leaves, packed
  • 4 cups (divided) — organic vegetable broth

Directions

  1. Preheat oven to 400°F (200°C). Line a large baking sheet with parchment paper and set aside.
  2. In a large bowl, combine the whole garlic cloves, roughly chopped onion, cauliflower florets, and broccoli florets.
  3. In a small bowl, add half the olive oil (about 1.5 tbsp / 20g) and mix in the dried parsley, basil, oregano, rosemary, thyme, and sea salt until combined into a spiced oil.
  4. Pour the spiced oil over the vegetables and toss until all pieces are well coated.
  5. Spread the coated vegetables evenly on the prepared baking sheet in a single layer. Roast for about 30 minutes, or until very tender and caramelized, flipping halfway through (around 15 minutes).
  6. When the vegetables are nearly done roasting (about 25 minutes in), warm the remaining olive oil (1.5 tbsp / 21g) in a large soup pot over medium heat.
  7. Add the kale leaves and half of the vegetable broth (2 cups / 473g) to the pot and cover with a lid. Let cook for 2 minutes, or until kale is wilted and tender.
  8. When the roasted vegetables are done, add them to the pot with the wilted kale. Pour in the remaining vegetable broth (2 cups / 473g).
  9. Bring the soup to a gentle boil over medium-high heat, then remove from heat immediately.
  10. Use an immersion blender to puree the soup directly in the pot until smooth and creamy. Alternatively, carefully transfer the soup in batches to a high-speed blender with the lid vent open (cover with a towel to prevent splattering) and blend until smooth.
  11. Taste and adjust seasoning with additional salt if needed. Divide into 4 serving bowls and enjoy!
Why this works
  • Roasting at 400°F triggers the Maillard reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars, creating hundreds of savory flavor compounds (pyrazines, furans).
  • Caramelization of garlic occurs at 300-350°F, converting harsh sulfur compounds into sweet, nutty flavors through enzymatic breakdown.
  • Whole garlic cloves roasted intact develop mellow sweetness as allicin (pungent compound) breaks down into milder polysulfides.
  • Olive oil's polyphenols infuse vegetables during roasting, adding fruity, peppery notes. Coating vegetables prevents moisture loss and promotes even browning.
  • Cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower) contain indole-3-carbinol and sulforaphane—compounds with cancer-preventative properties that survive roasting.
  • Roasting concentrates vegetable sugars through water evaporation, intensifying natural sweetness (especially in onions, cauliflower, broccoli).
  • Kale contains lutein and zeaxanthin (carotenoids) that are better absorbed with fat. Wilting kale in oil enhances nutrient bioavailability.
  • Quick wilting (2 minutes) preserves kale's vitamin C and folate, which degrade with prolonged heat exposure above 180°F.
  • Dark leafy greens like kale are high in fiber (7g per serving in this recipe), which supports gut health and may reduce cancer risk.
  • Blending roasted vegetables with greens creates an emulsion—microscopic particles suspended in liquid—resulting in a creamy, velvety texture without dairy.
  • Garlic and onions contain allium compounds (allicin, diallyl sulfide) that boost immune cells and may help prevent cancer development.
  • This recipe combines cancer-preventative foods: cruciferous vegetables (sulforaphane), alliums (organosulfur compounds), and dark leafy greens (antioxidants).

What these labels mean

Vegetarian means no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. Cheeses can use animal rennet, so check labels if that matters to you.

Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients at all, including dairy, eggs, honey, gelatin, and animal rennet. A vegetarian recipe is not automatically vegan.

These are guidance labels, not certification. Ingredient brands and processing aids vary.
