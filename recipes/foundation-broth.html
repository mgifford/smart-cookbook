Recipe

Foundation Broth

Source

Servings
4
Prep
2 hr
Dietary
Vegetarian: not recorded · Vegan: not recorded
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes

Ingredients

  • 250g (about 1/2 lb) — meat on the bone (any cut)
  • 600 ml (2.5 cups) — water
  • 30g (2 tbsp) — cereal (sago, rice or pearl barley), washed
  • 1 stalk — celery stalk, finely chopped
  • 1 medium carrot — carrot, peeled and finely chopped
  • 1 medium onion — onion, peeled and finely chopped
  • 1/2 tsp (to taste) — salt
  • 1/4 tsp (to taste) — black pepper

Directions

  1. Remove any visible fat from the meat and cut into small pieces, keeping the bones intact.
  2. Place the meat and bones in a large pot and cover with cold water. Let soak for 30 minutes (this draws out impurities and blood, resulting in a clearer broth).
  3. After soaking, bring the pot slowly to a boil over medium heat. Skim off any foam or scum that rises to the surface during the first 10 minutes of boiling.
  4. Once the soup is boiling, add the washed cereal (sago, rice, or pearl barley). Stir gently to prevent sticking.
  5. Reduce heat to low and simmer gently for 1 hour, stirring occasionally. The slow simmer extracts collagen and flavor from the bones.
  6. Add the finely chopped celery, carrot, and onion to the soup. Continue simmering for another 30 minutes until the vegetables are tender and the broth is flavorful.
  7. Remove the bones from the soup using a slotted spoon or strainer. Discard the bones.
  8. Taste and season the broth with salt and pepper to taste. Serve hot as a nourishing, easily digestible soup.
Why this works
  • Soaking meat and bones in cold water for 30 minutes draws out blood proteins and impurities, resulting in a clearer, cleaner-tasting broth.
  • Bringing broth to a boil slowly (not rapid boiling) prevents proteins from coagulating too quickly, which would cloud the broth.
  • Skimming foam (denatured proteins and impurities) from the surface during the first 10 minutes of boiling creates a crystal-clear broth.
  • Simmering at 180-200°F extracts collagen from bones and connective tissue. Collagen breaks down into gelatin, which gives broth body and richness.
  • Bones release minerals (calcium, phosphorus, magnesium) into the broth during long simmering, creating a nutrient-dense liquid.
  • Cereal starches (rice, barley, sago) gelatinize when heated in liquid, absorbing water and thickening the broth while adding texture.
  • Mirepoix vegetables (celery, carrot, onion) are the classic aromatic base. Their sugars caramelize slightly during simmering, adding depth.
  • Onions contain sulfur compounds that break down into sweet, savory molecules during cooking, balancing the broth's flavor.
  • Carrots add natural sweetness through sugars and beta-carotene (which also gives the broth a warm, golden color).
  • Celery contains glutamic acid (a natural source of umami), which enhances the savory depth of the broth.
  • This foundation broth is easily digestible and ideal for people recovering from illness, surgery, or cancer treatment—providing protein, minerals, and gentle calories.
  • Removing bones before serving prevents choking hazards and makes the soup easier to consume for those with swallowing difficulties.

What these labels mean

Vegetarian means no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. Cheeses can use animal rennet, so check labels if that matters to you.

Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients at all, including dairy, eggs, honey, gelatin, and animal rennet. A vegetarian recipe is not automatically vegan.

These are guidance labels, not certification. Ingredient brands and processing aids vary.
