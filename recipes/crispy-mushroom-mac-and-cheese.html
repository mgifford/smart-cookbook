Crispy Mushroom Mac and Cheese (Weeknight)

Proposed - not yet kitchen-tested

Servings
5
Prep
30 min
Cook
40 min
Dietary
Vegetarian: marked yes · Vegan: marked no
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes Cook’s notes

Ingredients

  • 350 g (about 3 1/2 cups) dry macaroni or small shells
  • 550 g (about 20 medium cremini mushrooms), sliced 5–6 mm thick
  • 1 medium yellow onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 250 g (about 2 1/4 cups) grated mature cheddar
  • 3 tbsp butter
  • about 1/3 cup all-purpose flour
  • 600 ml (2 1/2 cups) whole milk
  • 2 tsp Dijon mustard
  • about 3 thick slices (120 g) purchased garlic bread, cut into cubes
  • 1/4 cup finely grated Parmesan
  • 1 1/3 tbsp (18 g) olive oil, divided
  • 1 tsp (5 ml) soy sauce, optional
  • about 2/3 tsp fine sea salt, plus more for the pasta water
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

Directions

  1. Heat the oven to 210°C (410°F) with a rack in the middle. Remove any silicone handle cover from the skillet and do not use a glass lid in the oven. Lightly oil the skillet with about 1 tsp of the olive oil.
  2. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil. Add the macaroni and cook until about 2 minutes short of the package's al dente time. Reserve 120 ml (1/2 cup) pasta water, then drain without rinsing.
  3. While the water heats, slice the mushrooms 5–6 mm thick. Cut larger caps in half or quarters so the pieces stay substantial and meaty.
  4. Heat 1 1/2 tsp of the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add half the mushrooms in a single layer and cook 5–7 minutes without stirring for the first 2 minutes, then toss, until the released water has evaporated and the edges are deeply browned. Season lightly with a pinch of the salt, transfer to a bowl, and repeat with the remaining mushrooms and another 1 1/2 tsp oil.
  5. Add the onion to the same skillet with a splash of water if the pan is dry. Cook over medium heat for 5–6 minutes, scraping up the browned bits, until softened and golden. Add the garlic and cook for 30–45 seconds, until fragrant but not browned. Return the mushrooms to the pan, add the soy sauce if using, and toss. Transfer the mixture to a bowl and wipe the skillet if needed.
  6. Make the sauce: melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Whisk in the flour and cook for 1–2 minutes, until it smells nutty but has not darkened. Add the milk in a thin stream while whisking, then simmer gently for 4–5 minutes, whisking, until the sauce is thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
  7. Remove the sauce from the heat. Whisk in the Dijon, then add the grated cheddar a handful at a time, stirring until melted. Season with the pepper and a little of the remaining salt. If the sauce is very thick, whisk in pasta water 1 tbsp at a time; it should be loose enough to coat the pasta and still flow into the pan.
  8. While the sauce simmers, toast the garlic bread cubes on a small baking sheet at 210°C (410°F) for 5–6 minutes, until crisp and lightly golden. Set aside; the bread will finish browning on top of the pasta.
  9. Add the drained pasta and the mushroom mixture to the sauce and fold until evenly combined. Check that mushroom pieces are distributed throughout, and add pasta water a splash at a time if the mixture is stiff.
  10. Spread the mixture in the oiled skillet in an even layer about 2.5–3 cm (1–1.25 inches) deep. Scatter the Parmesan evenly over the top.
  11. For the bottom crust, if the skillet is stovetop-safe: set it over medium heat for 3–4 minutes, rotating it once or twice, until you hear gentle sizzling and the edge looks set. Lift an edge with a thin spatula to check for light browning. Do not leave the pan unattended. If the pan is not stovetop-safe, or you are unsure, skip this step and extend the bake by 5 minutes.
  12. Bake uncovered at 210°C (410°F) for 15 minutes. Scatter the toasted garlic bread over the top and bake for 8–10 minutes more, until the bread is golden and the sauce bubbles at the edges. For a deeper top colour, broil for 2–3 minutes only if the skillet is broiler-safe, watching constantly.
  13. Rest for 5 minutes. Slide a thin spatula under each portion and lift it with as much browned base as possible. The target is a golden, crunchy top, browned patches underneath, a creamy centre, and clearly identifiable mushroom pieces.
Why this works
  • Mushrooms release a lot of water. While that water is present, the surface stays near 100°C and the mushrooms steam; once it evaporates, the surface can get hot enough for Maillard browning and roasted flavours.
  • Browning is a family of reactions between amino acids and reducing sugars, not a single switch at one temperature. It typically becomes noticeable on a dry surface around 140–165°C (285–330°F), and the browned crust adds savoury, roasted notes.
  • Aged mature cheddar is drier and more savoury than mild cheddar, so 250 g carries the sauce without Gruyère, blue cheese, or truffle. The béchamel supplies fat and starch that keep the melted cheese from tightening.
  • Cheese is an emulsion of fat, water, and protein. Boiling, or adding cheese to a very hot sauce, can make the casein proteins clump and the fat leak out, giving a grainy sauce; residual heat is enough to melt cheddar.
  • Pasta keeps absorbing water in the oven. Cooking it about two minutes short of al dente leaves room for that absorption, so the finished dish stays creamy instead of collapsing into soft strands.
  • Bread is mostly starch and water. Toasting it first drives off water and sets a crisp structure; bread added raw at the start would absorb sauce and steam, losing the crunch.
  • A flat pan gives the pasta a continuous contact surface. Ridges add contact along their channels but can lift parts of the mixture away at the peaks; whether they make more crust is a test question here, not a settled rule.
  • Bottom browning needs contact, fat, heat, and a dry interface. A wet sauce keeps the base near 100°C until water evaporates, and cheese proteins can stick; moderate stovetop heat, a lightly greased pan, and a 5-minute rest improve the odds but do not guarantee a crisp base.
  • A shallower layer puts more of the mixture near the hot pan and lets moisture escape faster, which favours browning; a deeper layer insulates the centre and stays creamier. A 12-inch skillet gives roughly a 2.5–3 cm layer for this batch.
  • The starch that pasta releases into the cooking water helps the sauce cling and stabilises the cheese emulsion; a splash of pasta water can loosen a thick sauce without adding more fat.

Dietary notes

This recipe is marked vegetarian: it contains no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. This recipe is marked not vegan. Dairy is central to the current recipe, so it is not vegan. Cheeses may use animal rennet; check labels if that matters to you. A vegan adaptation would need plant milk, vegan butter, and a vegan melting cheese or a nutritional-yeast sauce; it has not been tested and will not behave identically.

What these labels mean
