Creamy Potato Soup (High Fibre)

Source

Servings
6
Prep
10 min
Cook
40 min
Dietary
Vegetarian: not recorded · Vegan: not recorded
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes

Ingredients

  • 3 tbsp — extra virgin olive oil
  • 1 large onion — white onion, chopped
  • 4 cloves — garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp — white wine vinegar
  • 350 ml (1.5 cups) — vegetable broth
  • 650g (about 3 medium potatoes) — white potato, roughly chopped
  • 200g (about 1 cup) — cannellini beans, tinned (drained and rinsed)
  • 1/2 tsp — Dijon mustard
  • 1 tbsp — fresh lemon juice
  • 1 tsp — smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp (to taste) — salt
  • 1/4 tsp (to taste) — black pepper
  • 1/2 cup — chives, chopped (for serving)
  • 6 tbsp (1.5 tbsp per serving) — Greek yoghurt (for serving)
  • 1 cup — shredded cheese (for serving)

Directions

  1. Heat 2 tablespoons (about 27g) of olive oil in a large pot over medium heat.
  2. Add the chopped onion and minced garlic, along with a large pinch of salt and pepper. Cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened and fragrant.
  3. Add the white wine vinegar and cook for approximately 30 seconds, stirring to deglaze any browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
  4. Add the roughly chopped potatoes, vegetable broth, and drained cannellini beans to the pot. Stir to combine.
  5. Bring the mixture to a boil over high heat, then reduce the heat to low and leave to simmer for about 30 minutes, or until the potatoes are completely tender and easily pierced with a fork.
  6. Remove from heat and let cool slightly (about 5 minutes). Transfer the soup mixture to a blender. Add the Dijon mustard, fresh lemon juice, smoked paprika, and the remaining 1 tablespoon (14g) of olive oil. Blend until completely smooth and creamy.
  7. Taste and season to perfection with additional salt and pepper as needed. If the soup is too thick, add a splash of broth or water to reach desired consistency.
  8. Divide the soup between 6 bowls. Top each serving with a dollop (1.5 tbsp) of Greek yoghurt, chopped chives, and shredded cheese.
Why this works
  • Heating olive oil at medium temperature releases fruity, peppery flavor compounds without breaking down beneficial polyphenols.
  • Sautéing onions and garlic activates enzymes that break down sulfur compounds into sweet, savory flavor molecules (thiosulfinates).
  • Vinegar's acetic acid deglazes the pot by dissolving browned proteins (fond), incorporating concentrated flavor back into the soup.
  • Potatoes contain amylose and amylopectin starches that swell when heated in liquid. When blended, these create a naturally creamy texture without dairy.
  • Cannellini beans are 25% starch by weight. When pureed, their starch granules and protein matrix create body and creaminess while adding 5g fiber per serving.
  • Simmering (180-200°F) gently breaks down cell walls without violent boiling, which can make potatoes waterlogged and bland.
  • Dijon mustard contains lecithin (from egg yolk), which acts as an emulsifier to keep fat and water molecules suspended together for silky texture.
  • Lemon juice's citric acid brightens flavors by stimulating taste receptors and balancing the earthy starchiness of potatoes.
  • Smoked paprika adds depth through compounds created during smoking process (phenols, guaiacol). Color comes from carotenoid pigments.
  • Blending hot soup creates friction heat that can increase temperature 20-30°F. Always blend carefully and vent steam to prevent eruption.
  • Greek yoghurt's lactic acid adds tanginess that contrasts with the soup's creaminess. Live cultures (probiotics) support gut health.

What these labels mean

Vegetarian means no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. Cheeses can use animal rennet, so check labels if that matters to you.

Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients at all, including dairy, eggs, honey, gelatin, and animal rennet. A vegetarian recipe is not automatically vegan.

These are guidance labels, not certification. Ingredient brands and processing aids vary.
