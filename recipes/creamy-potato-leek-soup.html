Recipe

Creamy Potato and Leek Soup

Source

Servings
6
Prep
40 min
Dietary
Vegetarian: not recorded · Vegan: not recorded
Ingredients Directions Why this works Dietary notes

Ingredients

  • 1-2 tsp — olive or vegetable oil
  • 2 medium leeks — leeks, sliced (white and light green parts)
  • 1 tsp — cumin seeds
  • 1 kg (about 4-5 medium potatoes) — potatoes, peeled and chopped
  • 5 cups — vegetable or chicken stock
  • 1/2 cup — cream (heavy cream or thickened cream)

Directions

  1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium heat. Add the sliced leeks and cook for 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally, until soft and translucent but not browned.
  2. Add the cumin seeds to the pan and cook for 2 minutes, stirring constantly. The cumin will become fragrant and toasted—this releases its essential oils and deepens the flavor.
  3. Add the chopped potatoes and stock to the pan. Stir to combine, then bring to a boil over high heat.
  4. Once boiling, reduce heat to medium-low and simmer for 25-30 minutes, or until the potatoes are completely tender and easily pierced with a fork.
  5. Remove from heat and let cool slightly (about 5 minutes). Puree the soup in a blender or food processor until completely smooth. Work in batches if necessary, filling the blender only halfway to prevent hot liquid from erupting.
  6. Return the pureed soup to the saucepan. Stir in the cream and heat gently over low heat until warmed through (do not boil after adding cream, as it may separate).
  7. Taste and adjust seasoning with salt and pepper if needed. Serve hot, garnished with fresh herbs or a swirl of cream if desired.
Why this works
  • Heating oil before adding leeks creates a barrier that prevents moisture loss, allowing leeks to soften without browning (sweating technique).
  • Leeks contain fructans (a type of carbohydrate) that break down during cooking, releasing natural sweetness and creating a mild, delicate onion flavor.
  • Toasting cumin seeds in hot oil activates volatile compounds (cuminaldehyde, pinene) that create warm, earthy, slightly citrus-like aromas.
  • Whole cumin seeds release flavor more slowly than ground cumin, providing a subtle, layered taste throughout the soup.
  • Potatoes contain 15-17% starch (amylose and amylopectin). When heated in liquid, starch granules swell and gelatinize, creating natural creaminess.
  • Simmering at 180-200°F gently breaks down potato cell walls without violent boiling, which can make potatoes waterlogged and bland.
  • Pureeing soup creates an emulsion—microscopic starch and vegetable particles suspended in liquid—that feels rich and velvety even before cream is added.
  • Cream contains 35-40% fat. Fat molecules coat the tongue, creating a luxurious mouthfeel and carrying fat-soluble flavors (like cumin's essential oils).
  • Adding cream off-heat or on very low heat prevents curdling. High heat can cause cream proteins to coagulate and separate from the liquid.
  • This soup provides comfort and easy-to-digest calories, ideal for people recovering from illness or undergoing cancer treatment.
  • Leeks are milder than onions, making them easier on sensitive digestive systems while still providing aromatic depth.

What these labels mean

Vegetarian means no meat, poultry, fish, or shellfish. Dairy and eggs may remain. Cheeses can use animal rennet, so check labels if that matters to you.

Vegan means no animal-derived ingredients at all, including dairy, eggs, honey, gelatin, and animal rennet. A vegetarian recipe is not automatically vegan.

These are guidance labels, not certification. Ingredient brands and processing aids vary.
